Drosophila melanogaster

The common fruit fly is a small insect, about three millimetres long, that has been one of biology's most studied organisms for more than a century. It walks with an alternating tripod gait: the front and hind legs on one side move together with the middle leg on the other.

Walking flies alternate runs with pauses and sharp turns. The flies on this page do the same, and they can be drawn toward the cursor or pushed away from it. Move the pointer around, or use the panel to change how they behave.

Larvae hatch from eggs laid on rotting fruit, feed for a few days, pupate, and emerge as adults about ten days after the egg was laid at room temperature. This short generation time is a large part of why the fly became a model organism.

Its genome is compact, its genetics are well mapped, and a whole adult brain has now been reconstructed at synaptic resolution. Simulated bodies such as NeuroMechFly combine that anatomy with physics to study how the nervous system produces walking.
